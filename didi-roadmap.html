How this list gets made

How we decide what's next

Two questions, answered every week. Neither one is a guess, because DiDi already records its own demand — every question it gets asked and can't answer yet is a real request, in a real person's words. The teams closest to customers own most of what follows.

First question — how a skill earns a spot

1 Questions DiDi couldn't answer

Support owns this. Every question DiDi gets and can't handle yet is already on record, in the words the person actually used. It is the highest-quality signal we have, and it costs nothing to collect.

2 What it would take to buy, or buy more

Sales owns this. The skills customers name in demos and renewals, tied to the account, so a nice-to-have is easy to tell apart from a dealbreaker.

3 What's holding up a rollout

Implementation owns this. The specific thing stopping a live account from opening DiDi up to more of their teams.

4 Rules we can't act on yet

Product owns this. When someone writes a rule DiDi can't carry out, that gap names the next skill to build. The misses are the roadmap.

Three of those four come straight from the customer-facing teams. They don't advise this list — they generate it. Each entry is one line: who asked, what they wanted, and what it's holding up. An outcome, not a spec.

Second question — what ships first

1 How many teams need it

One team asking is a request. Several teams asking for the same thing is the next skill we build.

2 Whether it wins or keeps business

Tied to a live deal, a renewal, or an account ready to expand. The customer-facing teams make this call, and it stands.

3 How much repeat work it removes

Weighed against the questions people answer by hand today. A skill that ends a recurring chore moves up.

4 How big the lift is

Some skills take under an hour. The biggest take a few. Anything that needs new access rules worked out is its own bucket, because that is the slow part.

5 Whether it changes anything

Questions ship quickly. Anything that changes your data waits for the review step, on purpose.

The default: whatever helps the most people for the least work goes first. That's what makes a steady two or three a week realistic rather than aspirational — a week is a couple of quick skills or one deeper workflow, so cheap and widely useful should win, and expensive should need a reason.

Two exceptions, both deliberate. One slot a week is reserved for trust and groundwork, because left to the scoring alone we would never finish the unglamorous work everything else depends on. And overrides are expected — said out loud, with a reason. The scoring orders the list; it doesn't make the call.

Every skill also ships with one more line: what should stop happening if this works. If a heads-up is doing its job, the same question stops getting asked by hand — and that answer feeds straight back into the top of this list, so each week's list is sharper than the last.

Also in development — underneath everything

Making DiDi rock-solid

Running in parallel with every skill above: right answer, right person, every time. A wrong answer given confidently is worse than no answer.

✓ Always finds the answer

Fix search so DiDi can reliably read your policies and past notes.

✓ Reaches a real manager

Urgent flags go to an actual supervisor — never a dead end, never a false "help is coming."

✓ Same question, same answer

Consistent results no matter who asks or how they word it.

✓ Only what you can see

Every reply stays inside your team and permissions — no leaks, no gaps.

✓ Clean, readable texts

Messages look right on a phone — no stray symbols or formatting.

✓ Remembers the right things

Keeps useful context, and never treats a guess as a fact.

✓ Fast for huge teams

Snappy even across thousands of sites — no timeouts.

✓ Shows its work

Answers are built from real data you can trace, not invented.

✓ Never promises what it can't do

If something can't be worked out, DiDi says so plainly — instead of saying a list is coming later and going quiet.

✓ Replies land in the right thread

Answers come back in the same email conversation you asked in, not as a stray new message.

✓ Changes are reviewed, not silent

When DiDi changes something, it arrives as a request a person approves, with a record of exactly what changed.

✓ Only the abilities you allow

Each company's DiDi carries only the skills that company turned on — enforced by the system, not by trust.
